The Friday That Disappeared, and the Plan That Saved the Next One

Nusrat's Fridays keep vanishing: a late wake-up, endless scrolling, chores, and suddenly it's Saturday night. Her friend Mitu has a three-part plan scribbled on a paper napkin. A Friday story about weekends that actually restore you.

A Friday story. The people and places are fictional; the lessons are real.

At 9 p.m. on a Friday in late November, Nusrat looked up from her phone and realised the whole day had gone.

She had woken at eleven. Scrolled until noon. Rushed to get ready before Jumu’ah, when her father and brother went to the mosque. Lunch with the family, a little more scrolling, then her mother needed help sorting winter clothes. Then a cousin visited. Then more scrolling.

She hadn’t rested. She hadn’t done anything she enjoyed. And tomorrow, Saturday, she had to go to the office for half a day.

Photo by igorovsyannykov on Pixabay (Pixabay Content License).

“Plan your weekend? That sounds like work”

On Saturday evening she complained to Mitu over a video call. Mitu was eating jhalmuri on her rooftop in Uttara.

“You need a weekend plan,” Mitu said.

“A plan? The whole point of the weekend is no plans.”

“No plan means your phone plans it for you,” Mitu said. “And your chores. And whoever visits.”

The napkin

The next Thursday, at Karim chacha’s tea stall, Mitu drew three boxes on a paper napkin.

“Three kinds of time,” she said. “Rest: nothing to achieve. Sleep, a slow breakfast, a walk. Connect: people you love. Grow: one thing that makes you a bit better: reading, drawing, exercise, cooking something new.”

“And chores?”

“One box. One hour. A fixed time, so they don’t eat the whole day.”

“That’s it?”

“Two or three anchors a day. The rest is white space. You don’t plan every hour. You just make sure the good things are in there.”

Thursday night, five minutes

That night, before bed, Nusrat opened her notebook and wrote:

Friday - Morning: sleep until 9, tea and paratha with Amma on the balcony (rest) - After Jumu’ah: family lunch, then call Nani (connect) - 4 p.m.: one hour of chores, winter clothes, done (admin) - Evening: walk around the lake with Mitu, then sketch for half an hour (grow + connect)

It took five minutes.

The Friday that stayed

Friday felt different from the moment she woke up. She knew what the day was for. She left her phone in her room during breakfast and noticed how her mother’s laugh filled the balcony. The chores, contained to an hour, didn’t spread. On the evening walk, Mitu told her about a new client and Nusrat laughed so hard a rickshaw-wala turned around.

At 10 p.m., she sketched for thirty minutes: the rooftop water tanks of Mirpur under a hazy moon.

On Saturday morning, she went to the office rested, for the first time in weeks.

Try this week

On Thursday evening, write one rest, one connect and one grow activity for Friday, plus a one-hour admin block. On Saturday night, ask yourself: did I rest? Did I spend time with people I care about? Did I do one thing for myself?

Summary

  • Unplanned weekends often disappear into chores and scrolling.
  • Use three kinds of time: rest, connect and grow.
  • Contain chores in one fixed admin block.
  • Keep it to two or three anchors a day, with white space.
  • Plan in five minutes on Thursday evening.
